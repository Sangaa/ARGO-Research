## Method lessons from TOTO and Claude research

These are methods, not mathematical results transferred into a financial model.

1. **Challenge the premise first.** Claude's R09 record checked the assumed collision mechanism before extending it and found the example did not support the proposed generalization. In this lane, validate source publication times, release vintages, and quote conventions before assigning predictive meaning.
2. **Use distinct checks and state scope.** The Singmaster record compares separate derivations, a fast search, and a bounded brute-force check while stating their limits. Here, compare the model, B0, and ablations on the same predeclared targets, and label a sampled replay as sampled.
3. **Make counts reproducible and retain corrections.** The TOTO/Claude R23 record preserves an off-by-one correction and moves population counts into a certificate. Keep verifier snapshots by source blob; future Gold Lab certificates should print their own sample and missingness counts.
4. **Separate software review from scientific validation.** A second provider or interpreter can challenge implementation evidence. It is not human peer review and cannot establish out-of-sample market performance.
5. **Keep misses and abstentions.** The P001 miss and unscorable Egypt outcome constrain the claims; do not hide them in later rounds.

The public research tree contains identifiable TOTO and Claude records for these lessons. No directly attributable HORUS gold-method review was located in the material used for this transfer; no finding is attributed to HORUS. That is a source gap, not an inferred endorsement.
