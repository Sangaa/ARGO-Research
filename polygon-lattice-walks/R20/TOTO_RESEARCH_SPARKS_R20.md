## Model and exact ambiguity
For a probability measure mu on R^d, let tilde(mu) be its reflection, and let X_n,Y_n,Z_n be independent n-step sums with iid increments mu. The distribution of X_n-Y_n is (mu*tilde(mu))^(*n). Choose two finite probability measures p,q and define mu=p*q, nu=p*tilde(q). Commutativity gives mu*tilde(mu)=nu*tilde(nu) exactly. Thus every equal-length independent pair difference agrees in law for every n>=1, including any function of that single difference, radial moments, exact and approximate collision probabilities and characteristic-function measurements. This does NOT assert equality of the entire joint law of measurements sharing a path or of unequal-length pairs.
The construction is established homometry; see Rosenblatt and Seymour, The Structure of Homometric Sets, DOI https://doi.org/10.1137/0603035 (abstract checked). The application to walk powers is an elementary convolution consequence.

## Explicit centered noncongruent example
Take p uniform on P={(0,0),(1,0),(0,2)}, q uniform on Q={(0,0),(4,0),(0,5)}. Direct sums and differences each have nine distinct points. Center each resulting law and multiply coordinates by three to stay integral. The resulting supports A and B are printed by the certificate. Centering cancels in pair differences; both receive the same scale. A has four convex-hull vertices, B has six, so they are not related by any Euclidean isometry or similarity. The difference law agrees at every n, while at n=5 the endpoint supports even have different cardinalities, 382 and 386. The latter is a finite exact observation, not an asymptotic claim.

## Restore information: triple differences
Measure the JOINT distribution of (X-Y,X-Z) for three independent copies with a common reference X. Two separate marginal distributions do not suffice. In the example, the event ((-15,0),(-15,15)) has probability zero for A and 1/729 for B at n=1. Moreover E[(X_x-Y_x)^2 (X_x-Z_x)] equals the centered third moment of the x-coordinate (expand using independence after centering). At n=1 it is 130 for A and -126 for B; for independent sums of n steps the difference is 256*n by additivity of centered third moments. This provides a single explicit scalar separator for these two models, not for every possible pair.

## General uniqueness up to translation
For finite-support real probability laws mu,nu on R^d, equality of the joint triple difference distribution implies the laws differ only by translation. This is an established triple-correlation uniqueness principle for bounded support; see Uniqueness properties of higher-order autocorrelation functions, https://opg.optica.org/josaa/abstract.cfm?uri=josaa-9-3-388 (abstract checked). Here is a short proof for finite probability laws using real moment-generating functions instead of complex phase reconstruction.
Let M_mu(s)=E exp(s dot X), strictly positive and finite for every real s. The joint triple-difference MGF is M_mu(s+t)M_mu(-s)M_mu(-t). Equality for mu and nu gives g(s+t)+g(-s)+g(-t)=0 for g=log(M_nu/M_mu). Setting t=0, g(0)=0 and g(-s)=-g(s). Hence g(s+t)=g(s)+g(t); continuity implies g(s)=a dot s for a fixed vector a. Therefore M_nu(s)=exp(a dot s)M_mu(s), which identifies nu as the translate of mu by a via MGF uniqueness. Conversely translations cancel. For n-step walks of a known common n, the endpoint MGF is the step MGF raised to n; positive nth roots give the step law up to translation as well. Centering fixes translation. The statement uses full vector-valued joint triple data in a fixed coordinate frame; not merely triangle side lengths, noisy finite samples or arbitrary unbounded laws. No uniform stability guarantee.
