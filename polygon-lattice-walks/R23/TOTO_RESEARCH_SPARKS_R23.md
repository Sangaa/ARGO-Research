## Exact anchor-known conditioning
Fix known labeled anchors A=(0,0), B=(L,0), C=(c,h) with L>0, 0<c<L, h>0. Let d_i be the squared distances from unknown (x,y) to anchors i=0,1,2. Subtract squared range equations:
x=(d_0-d_1+L^2)/(2L),
y=(d_0-d_2+c^2+h^2-2cx)/(2h).
If measured squared distances are d_i+e_i with |e_i|<=eta, the linear inverse errors satisfy
dx=(e_0-e_1)/(2L),
dy=((1-c/L)e_0+(c/L)e_1-e_2)/(2h).
Hence |dx|<=eta/L and |dy|<=eta/h. The latter is sharp for this inverse over the independent bounded-error cube (e_0=e_1=eta,e_2=-eta), whether or not the noisy readings correspond to a physical exact location. This is not a claim of minimax optimality for all algorithms. The units of eta are squared length. If ordinary range error is <=delta and true ranges <=R then eta<=2R*delta+delta^2. No uncertainty in anchor coordinates is included.

## Separated signature, poorly conditioned inverse
Let L=2,c=1/2,0<h<=1/8 and P+=(3/4,1/4), P-=(3/4,-1/4). Consider S+={A,B,C,P+}, S-={A,B,C,P-}. A,B is the unique pair at squared distance 4 (all other squared distances <=9/4+1/64). The sorted squared side signature of ABC is (1/4+h^2,9/4+h^2,4). The other triangle sharing AB has minimum squared side 5/8, separated by >=23/64. Triangles not sharing AB have maximum squared side <=9/4+1/64, separated by >23/64. Thus the unique scalene anchor ABC has a recognition margin gamma>=23/64 in max norm for both sets, uniformly as h->0. Here gamma is distance to the nearest other unordered distinct triangle signature. Sorting is nonexpansive in max norm. Given an exact reference signature, candidate signatures with per-squared-side errors <=eta<gamma/2 can be classified by a threshold between eta and gamma-eta, provided candidates are distinct-point triangles and the noise bound applies. This does not solve noisy vertex labeling or discovering an unknown anchor.
The two P positions are distance 1/2 apart in the fixed anchor frame. Their three squared distance readings differ only in the last entry, by h, since (1/4+h)^2-(1/4-h)^2=h. Their midpoint readings therefore fit both with errors <=h/2. Any estimator using these readings and the same known anchor frame must incur position error >=1/4 on at least one of the two cases. This gives a genuine feasible-data ambiguity under bounded additive noise, separately from the sharp linear-inverse gain. It is an anchored-location result, NOT a constant lower bound on unlabeled shape error modulo isometries: reflecting the whole set in AB would move C by only 2h. That alternative alignment must not be silently excluded in a global shape metric.
For S+ choose ABP+ as an alternative anchor; its height is 1/4 and linear vertical gain 4. It is scalene and unique: it contains the unique AB edge and has a different signature from ABC. Hence both anchors are identifying in the exact R22 sense, but their inverse conditioning differs arbitrarily as h decreases. The conditional inverse with known exact anchors is not a full noisy distance-deck reconstruction algorithm.

## Sampling cost of a rare reference
For a uniform finite set with N points and a unique unordered scalene triangle, three iid draws (with replacement) match that triangle in any ordering with probability 6/N^3. Conditional on this event, a fourth independent draw is uniform on the N points. The mean number of independent quadruple draws before the first anchor event is N^3/6. For any particular target point, the probability of a successful reference and that point in the fourth draw is 6/N^4. The probability of missing at least one of the N targets after K independent quadruple draws is at most N*(1-6/N^4)^K <= N exp(-6K/N^4). Thus K>=ceil((N^4/6) log(N/delta)) suffices for all targets to appear with probability >=1-delta in this particular anchor-gated procedure. This is a conservative sufficient bound, not a necessary sample complexity, nor a lower bound for alternative algorithms or reusable overlapping observations. Assumes known exact anchor signature and reliable labeling, no measurement noise, independent quadruples. The event count can be improved using multiple compatible references, but no theorem about that here.

## Margin
A reference should be evaluated along at least three axes: signature separation, inverse conditioning and event availability. Do not collapse them into a universal score without a noise and sampling model. Next consider full distance-matrix stability modulo isometry, where near-collinear reflection can make global shape error much smaller than anchored-coordinate error, or exploit multiple references to reduce the rare-anchor cost.
