## Simultaneously optimal path in this family
Define layer n=j+l, the number of identities from the target selection. Any single replacement changes n by at most one, so every path from n=0 to n=k visits every layer. At a fixed n the horizontal coordinate x=r-ne is the same, while the vertical coordinate is n/(2q) in magnitude for j=0, and (k-n)/(2q) for j=1 (where the state exists). Thus the minimum required support in layer n is exactly
z_n=max(0,[min(n,k-n)^2/(4q^2)-kne+n^2e^2]/[k-ne]).
At endpoints this is zero. At all internal layers it is strictly positive by R27, since every nonempty proper replacement worsens the unassisted score. All paths have P>=max_n z_n, C>=sum_n z_n and L>=k-1=q. The latter counts state visits, not elapsed clock time. These three bounds are attained by the same path: first add m=floor(k/2)=ceil(q/2) c identities replacing a, then insert b in place of a, then the remaining c. For n<=m this uses j=0 and for n>=m+1 uses j=1. It visits exactly one cheapest state per layer. Thus no assumption of monotonicity needed for the lower bounds, even with arbitrary backtracking. Peak equals R30's w*=W(0,m). All internal states require some positive assistance: support cannot be set to zero at any interior visit on this model's path, though its magnitude can be reduced. A constant full-strength source held throughout is not the same as a variable-strength source; the latter may be unavailable.

## Asymptotic capacity consumption
Let C_q=sum_(n=1)^q z_n and P_q=max z_n. With t=n/k, the uniform approximation k*z_n=(1/4) min(t,1-t)^2+o(1) follows from k/q->1, n e=O(q^-3), and k n e=O(q^-2), with denominator k-ne and clipping at zero (nonexpansive). Hence the Riemann sum C_q tends to (1/4)*integral_0^1 min(t,1-t)^2 dt=1/48. Also q P_q->1/16 by R30 or uniform approximation and peak near t=1/2. Therefore C_q/(q P_q)->1/3. The result is a roughly two-thirds reduction in cumulative directional trace per equal-duration visit relative to constant peak trace on the q required active visits. Peak capacity does not fall: it is still P_q. Absolute C_q has a finite positive limit even though P_q tends to zero, since path length grows. Since final improvement G=1/(8q^3), C_q/G ~ q^3/6 (unit-visit aggregate compared to per-state score improvement; not an economic payback period absent a time and value model).

## General path and reuse boundaries
Minimax and sum objectives need not share an optimizer on general graphs: one route has three intermediate deficits of 2, another has one deficit of 3. Minimum peak is 2 (sum 6) while minimum sum is 3 (peak 3). This is an abstract graph example, not asserted to be realizable by R27's equal-trace group pool. For a fixed path with nonnegative state deficits, no ramp constraint, per-unit usage proportional to added trace, and no activation penalty, the least cumulative support is the sum of pointwise required support; removing it at a zero-deficit state is safe for that state. For serial nonoverlapping paths whose endpoint/start states meet their respective floors without support, a reusable capacity is governed by the maximum of their peak requirements rather than their sum, provided its direction/geometry is compatible and it can be redeployed freely. Cumulative usage still adds. This elementary scheduling observation is conditional, not proof of reuse in a physical or organizational system. No switching cost, ramp rate, varying dwell times, noisy estimates, real resource transfer or simultaneous demands are tested. If these matter the graph and costs must be enlarged.
