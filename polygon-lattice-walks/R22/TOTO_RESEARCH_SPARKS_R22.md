## Known upper bounds: do not rediscover or misstate
Pebody, Radcliffe and Scott, Finite Subsets of the Plane are 18-Reconstructible, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 16 (2003), 262–275, DOI https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895480101391648, author PDF https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/scott/Papers/plane.pdf. Read definitions, Theorem 1, Theorem 5 and section 5 via extracted text, with page 2 visually inspected. The paper uses orientation-preserving rigid motions, not reflections, for its 18-deck. Section 5 reports the doubling bound for reconstruction up to isometry. Alex Scott's Geometric reconstruction report in Oberwolfach Reports 2022 independently states that 36 suffices for finite planar sets up to isometry: https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46939?nt=1 . These are published bounds, not our discoveries, and no optimality is claimed. The paper also treats 6-decks on the circle under rotation; reflection-invariant distance measurements are a different observable.
For a uniform finite set S, the joint labeled iid distance-matrix law at order k determines its size N because Pr(d12=0)=1/N (k>=2), and each marginal at order j<=k. Restrict to all-distinct draws and forget labels; the resulting shape probabilities are exactly j!/N^j times the multiplicity of each congruence class of j-subsets. Thus the known 36-deck theorem applies to our exact distance observation of uniform finite planar sets. This argument does not establish the same bound for arbitrary real weights, continuous supports, noisy data, or unknown calibration. In particular, the unbounded-order hypothesis in R21 is rejected for the uniform finite planar class.

## New restricted derivation: a unique scalene triangle gives order four
Let S be a uniform finite planar point set with a noncollinear scalene triangle whose congruence class occurs exactly once among unordered distinct triples. With the exact joint labeled four-point distance law, infer N from the two-point marginal. The three-point marginal identifies that triangle's occurrence count; any rival uniform set with the same data also has precisely one such triangle. Choose a labeling of its distinct side lengths to identify its vertices uniquely. Condition the first three iid points to have these labeled distances. For each possible fourth point record the three distances to the anchors. Fix the triangle in a reference plane; the differences of squared distances to the three noncollinear centers are two independent linear equations for the fourth point's coordinates. Hence the conditional distribution recovers the whole uniform law in this reference frame, up to an overall isometry. Four observations suffice for this restricted class, no requirement that the fourth point be distinct. This is a short trilateration argument, not a claim of novelty.

## New restricted derivation: a unique diameter gives order five
Let S be a uniform finite planar set of at least two points with precisely one unordered pair at maximum distance D>0. Its size and unique diameter are determined by the two-point distance law. Any rival uniform set sharing that law has the same size and exactly one diameter pair. Fix those anchors at (-D/2,0),(D/2,0). Condition the first two of the iid draws to have distance D. The anchor ordering is equally likely in either direction and the remaining r draws remain iid from the original distribution.

A full labeled distance matrix determines the entire configuration up to isometry (e.g. the centered Gram matrix is recovered by double centering squared distances). Once the two ordered anchors are placed at their reference coordinates, the only remaining freedom is simultaneous reflection of the remaining coordinates in the anchor line, even when all points are collinear. Average over this freedom. Anchor reversal gives the other reflection of the reference axes up to the first reflection. Consequently the conditional distance law determines the symmetrized coordinate tensor
T_r = (1/4) sum_(g in H) (g*mu)^(tensor r),
where H consists of the four sign changes of the two coordinates, mu is the finite probability vector of the anchored set, and g*mu is its pushforward. This uses joint distance matrices, not separate distance marginals. Orders r=1,2,3 are available from at most five iid draws by marginalization.

For completeness, prove these three invariant tensors identify any finite probability vector mu up to H. Work in the finite-dimensional real vector space on the union of the H-orbits of the two candidate supports. The four characters of H=Z2 x Z2 take values +/-1. For each character c define v_c=(1/4)sum_g c(g) g*mu; then mu=sum_c v_c and g*v_c=c(g)v_c. The character projection of T_r in each tensor factor is zero unless the product of the r characters is trivial, in which case it is exactly the tensor product of their v components. T_1 gives the trivial component v_0. T_2 gives each v_c tensor v_c, determining v_c up to a sign (including exact detection of zero). If all three nontrivial components are nonzero, T_3 projected to their three distinct characters yields v_1 tensor v_2 tensor v_3; equality forces the product of the three relative signs to be +1. Exactly those sign patterns are realized by H, since c_3=c_1*c_2. If one or more components are zero, any signs on the remaining at most two nontrivial components are realizable by H (any distinct pair of nontrivial characters is independent). Thus identical T_1,T_2,T_3 imply the two anchored laws differ by a member of H only. This proves order five suffices for uniform planar finite sets with a unique diameter. It also applies within the known-unique-diameter class of finite weighted laws, as opposite anchor orders have equal probability, but the uniform-law argument for inferring the unique-pair property from the marginal does not transfer to unrestricted weighted rivals.
No universal five-point sufficiency or sharpness claimed.

## Margin
Known universal bound changes the question from unboundedness to geometry-dependent reconstruction order. Unique anchors can greatly reduce the bound; residual symmetry group determines which mixed tensor information is needed. Next compare symmetry-breaking anchors and statistical conditioning: a rare unique triangle or diameter event may be difficult to estimate. No independent review, physical experiment, sample complexity or novelty assessment.
