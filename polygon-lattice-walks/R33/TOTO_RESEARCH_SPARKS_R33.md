## Causal least schedule on a tree
Each node v represents the information history at a unit-spaced time. The finite rooted tree and all possible branches are known, but which branch occurs is unknown before its revelation. Decisions are made after observing the node, not before. A single real support value w(v) is shared by all scenarios passing through v. Given z(v)>=0, require w(v)>=z(v) at every node, and for each parent p and child v require -d<=w(v)-w(p)<=u, u,d>=0. The root and all leaves must have zero support; there may also be a uniform cap K. No ramp delay other than these edge bounds, no switching costs, no endogenous information acquisition.
Backward recursion L(v)=max(z(v), max_children(L(child)-u)). Equivalently L(v) is the max of z(x)-u dist(v,x) over descendants including v. Every feasible policy dominates L since it must be able to reach every possible descendant demand with limited increase. Forward recursion w(root)=L(root), w(v)=max(L(v),w(parent)-d). This gives w>=z and respects the decrease bound. Also L(v)<=L(parent)+u<=w(parent)+u, so increase bound holds. By induction any feasible policy dominates this w pointwise. Hence feasibility with cap and zero endpoints is equivalent to the computed w respecting them; when feasible it minimizes any objective nondecreasing in each node value, including expected cumulative support with nonnegative scenario probabilities, without choosing the future branch in advance. O(number of nodes) arithmetic via two passes with parents indexed before children. This is not a bit-complexity bound or a general stochastic-control solution. All modeled scenarios are mandatory regardless of their probability; if chance constraints permit shortfalls, the optimum can differ. Forecast errors or missing branches can invalidate robust feasibility.

## Exact pulse example: value of perfect advance warning
Take symmetric up/down rate rho>0, potential one-time demand H=m*rho for integer m>=1 at time T, probability p in (0,1); otherwise demand is zero throughout. Cap >=H. At time a=T-ell an infallible announcement reveals which branch occurs, before the decision at that time. Assume ell nonnegative integer, a>=1, T>=m, and horizon >=T+m. Root support zero. All times unit-spaced. Until a-1 both scenarios share a history. Perfect information from the start would give the pulse branch tent rho*max(0,m-|t-T|) with total rho*m^2, and the no-event branch zero. The least causal policy has exactly the same support on the pulse branch: any causal policy must cover the pulse, and its path-specific least tent is compatible with the shared-history constraints. At the last common node a-1 its value is rho*n, n=max(0,m-ell-1). On the no-event branch the forward recursion releases at rate rho immediately after announcement. The no-event support is consequently the discrete tent rho*max(0,n-|t-(a-1)|), total rho*n^2, including the shared preparation. This follows from backward rate constraints to T and forward decrease constraints after revelation; both tents fit the horizon. Expected cumulative support p*rho*m^2+(1-p)*rho*n^2. The excess over perfect-information-from-start expected support is C_extra=(1-p)*rho*max(0,m-ell-1)^2. The value of increasing lead from ell to ell+1 is (1-p)*rho*(2n-1) if n>=1, otherwise zero. Lead ell>=m-1 removes this excess. The minus one reflects the decision occurring after revelation, with one rate step available at that same time from the preceding node; it is not an assertion about continuous-time warnings. Even without the event, unused reserve is a modeled cost of guarding against a possible demand, not necessarily waste in a real system. Costs here are support units summed over unit-duration visits, not currency or energy. No monetary value of information inferred. Capacity and rate identical across comparisons.

## Margin
A reliable warning may save cumulative reserve even when peak and ramp remain unchanged. Earlier information has diminishing incremental benefit for this pulse model, but not necessarily for general scenarios. Distinguish information availability, correctness and coverage: a noisy forecast with no excluded scenarios can leave worst-case reserve unchanged.
