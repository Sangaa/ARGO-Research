## Model
Let zeta=exp(2*pi*i/5), O=Z[zeta], K=Q(zeta)=Q(zeta_10), rho=1-zeta and phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2. The five-step direction set is {zeta^j: j=0..4}; the ten-direction set is its union with its negative. All steps are equiprobable and unit length. Observe the difference X_n-Y_n of independent n-step walks, not the single endpoint or the 2n-step pentagon walk (the pentagon's step law is not symmetric).
The pentagon step differences generate I=rho*O: adjacent differences are rho*zeta^j, spanning I; all differences belong to I. O/I=F_5 by evaluation at zeta=1 mod 5. Every pentagon endpoint at time n is in the coset n+I. The decagon step differences generate O: they include generators of I and 2; since 2 is nonzero in F_5, the generated additive subgroup is O. Thus the difference processes (sums of n iid pair differences) have full difference lattices I and O respectively. They include zero as an increment and are aperiodic in those lattices. These are asymptotic lattice supports, not assertions of reachability of all points at each finite n.

## Norm spectrum and ambiguity
For w in O use embeddings z(w),z'(w) corresponding to zeta and zeta^2. Define N(w)=|z(w)|^2*|z'(w)|^2, the positive integer norm for w!=0. Norm(rho)=5. Multiplication by rho gives a bijection O->I, commutes with multiplication by phi^j for every integer j, and multiplies N by 5. Phi is a unit: phi=-(zeta^2+zeta^3), real quadratic norm -1. Hence for counts of nonzero orbits modulo <phi> (rotations not quotiented out), r_5(5N)=r_10(N) for all positive integers N, and r_5(N)=0 if 5 does not divide N. This identity does NOT require class number one. Raw spectra are different, but normalizing N by the smallest nonzero norm makes them identical; the two specified geometries cannot be distinguished using only that normalized spectrum.

For the absolute counts use classical cyclotomic laws. The discriminant of K is 125, and the Minkowski bound 3*sqrt(125)/(2*pi^2)<2 gives class number one. Any unit h has h*bar(h) totally positive in the real quadratic integer ring Z[phi], of norm 1. Multiply this positive value by phi^(-2j) to enter [1,phi^2). Its trace x+1/x is an integer in [2,3), hence equals 2 and x=1. Therefore h/phi^j has both embedded moduli 1 and is a root of unity (all powers belong to a bounded discrete lattice, hence repeat). The roots of unity are exactly mu_10: a larger group would contain a primitive root of order a proper multiple of 10, with totient dividing 4, impossible. Thus the full unit group is mu_10 times <phi>. All ideals are principal, giving r_10(N)=10*a_K(N) where a_K(N) counts ideals of norm N. The prime 5 is totally ramified, residue degree 1; for p!=5 let f=ord_5(p), g=4/f. The coefficient factor at p^e is zero unless f divides e, and otherwise binom(e/f+g-1,g-1). At p=5 the factor is 1. Multiply factors and then 10. Classical background consulted: https://openmaths.org/courses/NT-ANT/cyclotomic-fields.html and https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Cyclotomic_field (search extracts). These splitting laws are established, not a new theorem about primes.

## Complete direct norm control
For coordinates k=(a,b,c,d) in the basis 1,zeta,zeta^2,zeta^3, write |z|^2=(A+B*sqrt(5))/2, |z'|^2=(A-B*sqrt(5))/2. Here A=2*sum(k_i^2)-sum_(i<j) k_i*k_j and B=ab+bc+cd-ac-ad-bd. Each phi-orbit has a unique representative 1<=|z|/|z'|<phi^2. Equivalently B>=0, 3B<A. Then N=(A^2-5B^2)/4>A^2/9 and A=(5*||k||^2-(sum k)^2)/2>=||k||^2/2. Thus ||k||^2<6sqrt(N), and every representative with N<=CAP lies in the box of radius floor((36*CAP)^(1/4)). Membership in I is sum(k)=0 mod 5. Direct enumeration does not use the prime formula or the rescaling identity. The rho map on coordinates is (a+d,b-a+d,c-b+d,2d-c); multiplication scales the two squared moduli by (5-sqrt(5))/2 and (5+sqrt(5))/2 respectively. Code verifies the equivalent exact integer form transformations.

## Complementary observation: collision probabilities distinguish the models
In the two complex embeddings the direction harmonics 1 and 2 have covariance I_4/2 for each original step (both polygons), as follows by summing roots of unity; the pair difference has covariance I_4. The image lattice of O has covolume sqrt(125)/4, and I has five times this covolume. For the pair-difference walk the only modulus-one character on the respective lattice torus is trivial: its support includes zero and generates the lattice. Fourier inversion near zero yields the standard full-rank aperiodic lattice local limit formula with covariance n*I_4; outside a neighborhood the characteristic function is bounded strictly below one. In particular the exact-collision probabilities satisfy
C_10(n) ~ 5sqrt(5)/(16*pi^2*n^2),
C_5(n) ~ 25sqrt(5)/(16*pi^2*n^2),
C_5(n)/C_10(n) -> 5.
This is a model-specific application of established LLT, background chapter https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/random-walk-a-modern-introduction/local-central-limit-theorem/2D2908A7742FFBEF09AFDA3E1F314582 (metadata checked). Finite n coefficients counted independently by direct dynamic programming of the two original walks, and collision probability calculated as sum of squared endpoint probabilities; no assumption that the pentagon is symmetric. At n=1 the ratio is 2, at n=10 it is about 4.871027695. No general monotonicity or finite-n convergence rate asserted.

## Further exact identity of the limiting observation profiles
For L=O or I define the Gaussian lattice function F_L(t;beta)=sum_(w in L) exp[-(beta*t^2*|z(w)|^2+t^(-2)*|z'(w)|^2)/2], beta>0. This is the R13 profile with an observation weight parameter. As in R13, for these pair-difference walks the local limit formula and Gaussian envelope (split n increments into two halves, bound local mass and Hoeffding tails) give the limiting normalized Gaussian observable along scale phases t=sqrt(n)/phi^floor(log_phi(sqrt(n))). The covariance above supplies the same two embedding coefficients. This derivation concerns asymptotic profiles, not measured finite-n Gaussian responses.
Write r1=|z(rho)|, r2=|z'(rho)|. Then r1^2*r2^2=5, and substitution w=rho*v gives the exact identity
F_I(t;beta)=F_O(t/r2;5*beta).
Both are log-periodic under multiplication by phi, by reindexing units. Averaging a full log period therefore gives M_I(beta)=M_O(5*beta). Thus even the entire limiting Gaussian profile can be made identical by a phase shift and a change in the observation parameter. At the same calibrated beta and phase, equality is not claimed. This identity does not imply identical finite-time dynamics. It is a second, analytic demonstration of why unspecified observation calibration is relevant to inverse identification.
