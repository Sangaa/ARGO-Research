## The phase-balanced protocol
Use R13 notation: lambda=1+sqrt(2), T=log(lambda), E even parity lattice, two embeddings z,z', and G(n) the normalized Gaussian observation ratio. Let f(s)=F(exp(s)), an analytic T-periodic function. R13 proves G(n)-f((log n)/2)->0 as n->infinity (periodicity handles phase wrapping).
Define the exact mean M=(1/T) integral_0^T f(s) ds. For any starting phase s, averaging f(s+v) over v uniform in [0,T] gives M. Equivalently the time-mixed observable (1/T) integral_0^T G(floor(N exp(2v))) dv tends to M as integer N->infinity. Proof: the R13 error tends uniformly to zero over n>=N, and integer rounding changes log(n)/2 by O(1/N) uniformly, so uniform continuity of f controls the remaining error. This is a mathematical mixture of normalized observables at different times; it is not a single-time detector or a raw collision probability. It presumes the exact-return normalization is available.

For m>=1, use n_j=floor(N lambda^(2j/m)), j=0..m-1, and H_m(N)=mean_j G(n_j). For each fixed m the same reasoning gives H_m(N)-f_m((log N)/2)->0, where f_m(s)=mean_j f(s+jT/m). Unlike continuous averaging, finite m need not exactly eliminate phase.

## Certified uniform error for the limiting profile
R13 gives the holomorphic sum
f(s)=sum_(w in E) exp[-(exp(2s)|z(w)|^2+exp(-2s)|z'(w)|^2)/2]
for |Im(s)|<pi/4. Periodicity and reflection hold throughout this strip by analytic continuation. On |Im(s)|<=pi/8, use them to reduce Re(s) to [0,T/2]. The real part of the exponent magnitude is at least cos(pi/4)/lambda * ||w||^2 > ||w||^2/4, by the R12 norm identity. Thus |f(s)| <= (sum_(k in Z) exp(-k^2/4))^4 <= (1+2 sqrt(pi))^4 <625, using integral comparison for the decreasing Gaussian.
The Fourier coefficient c_l has |c_l| <=625 exp(-2 pi |l| (pi/8)/T), by shifting the integration contour in the direction of exponential decay; periodic vertical sides cancel. The exponent factor pi^2/(4T)>2.7 and exp(2.7)>14, so |c_l|<=625*14^(-|l|).
The average of m equally spaced phase shifts cancels all Fourier modes except multiples of m (finite geometric sum). The constant coefficient is M. Consequently for EVERY real s,
|f_m(s)-M| <= 1250/(14^m-1).
This also bounds limsup_(N->infinity)|H_m(N)-M|. For m=8 the bound is about 8.4700452e-7; for m=12 about 2.2048223e-11. These bounds concern limiting phase bias, NOT a finite-N error bound or measurement noise. This is the established exponential convergence of the periodic trapezoid rule, applied with an explicit model-specific strip bound. Background: Trefethen and Weideman, The Exponentially Convergent Trapezoidal Rule, SIAM Review (2014), https://doi.org/10.1137/130932132 . Source metadata/abstract checked; proof above is self-contained.

## Exact mean as an orbit sum
The unit group generated by u=lambda^(-1)=sqrt(2)-1 acts freely on E without zero. Multiplication by u scales |z| by u and |z'| by lambda. Each orbit therefore has a unique representative w satisfying 1<=|z(w)|/|z'(w)|<lambda^2. Zero contributes 1. For a representative set R, sum the positive integrands of each orbit and integrate a full log period; by Tonelli and unfolding, this is an integral over the entire real s-axis for that representative:
integral_R exp[-(e^(2s) A+e^(-2s) B)/2] ds,
where A=|z(w)|^2>0, B=|z'(w)|^2>0. Shift and change variable v=2s+log(A/B)/2 to get (1/2) integral_R exp(-sqrt(AB) cosh(v)) dv = K_0(sqrt(AB)). Here K_0 is the standard modified Bessel function, NIST DLMF 10.32.9 https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.32.E9 (integral formula checked).
Hence
M = 1 + (1/T) sum_(w in R) K_0(|z(w)||z'(w)|).
The argument squared is the positive integer algebraic norm A*B. This is a second expression for the same mean; it is not a claim of a closed elementary value. The orbit count is for the subgroup generated by u only; other symmetries are not quotiented out. Convergence follows from nonnegative unfolding and the bounded integral of f.

## Margin
- Phase-sensitive and phase-averaged observables are different information products, not different physical systems.
- The log spacing is part of the protocol; uniform spacing in n is not a uniform phase average.
- The orbit formula reorganizes a Gaussian lattice sum into an arithmetic norm spectrum with Bessel weights. Next ask how its norm multiplicities are related to factorization, and whether other cyclotomic geometries supply different period groups.
- Do not infer that a zero displayed spread proves exact finite-node cancellation.
