## Non-identifiability from the admitted region
A fixed finite collection contains a accepted items and r rejected items, a,r>0. Independent binary truth labels are fixed, but only the a accepted labels are visible, y of them positive. Let z of the r rejected labels be positive, unknown. The incumbent accepts the accepted set and rejects the rest; compare an alternative accepting all items. Under unit misclassification loss their error counts are a-y+z and a-y+r-z. The alternative-minus-incumbent difference r-2z changes sign between z=0 and z=r. Both completions have the same entire observed accepted-label record, counts and rule decisions. Repeated reviews using just that record cannot distinguish the worlds or establish which rule is better. This is an elementary observational equivalence argument, not a claim that every self-review fails. Additional assumptions or new independent observations can break the equivalence. Example a=y=80,r=20: admitted precision is 100% in both worlds while incumbent full accuracy is 100% vs 80%. This is one pair of extreme completions, not empirical prevalence.

## Exact finite audit budget
Let a fixed rejected region have N identities, d>=1 of which carry a specified detectable defect. Audit a uniformly random subset of size m without replacement, independently of defect locations. Assume perfect external labels on audited items, a predeclared fixed sampling frame, no new defects during sampling. The probability of missing every defect is
P_miss = C(N-d,m)/C(N,m) = C(N-m,d)/C(N,d),
with zero when m>N-d. Thus m_min is the least integer m with this probability <= alpha. If there are at least d defects, the same bound applies by monotonicity. A deterministic review of m fixed identities can miss all d whenever m<=N-d. Uniform sampling is minimax for the stated detection objective: against a uniformly random d-subset of defect locations, any deterministic adaptive strategy's all-negative history fixes its inspection sequence; at most m distinct inspections leaves average miss probability >= C(N-m,d)/C(N,d). A randomized strategy is a mixture of deterministic strategies with independent random seeds, so its average miss probability is at least the same and some placement attains at least that average. Uniform sampling without replacement has the same miss probability for every placement and attains the bound. This does not prove optimality for heterogeneous costs, imperfect audits, targeted defect priors or interventions that alter labels. An audit with no detected defects is not proof of zero defects and the 95% detection guarantee conditional on d or more defects is not a posterior 95% probability of correctness.

## Rule revision versus automatic replacement
The defect discovery step identifies a failure candidate; it does not establish that a replacement rule is superior. The sign r-2z in the toy comparison explicitly requires knowledge of z, not merely detection of one defect. Before revision, use separately specified loss, independent representative evaluation across both decision regions, preserved rule versions and failure evidence. A stable rule can be retained when the evidence does not justify replacement. No operational rule change or claim that ARGO already satisfies an audit guarantee. User intent about revisability is preserved as provenance.
