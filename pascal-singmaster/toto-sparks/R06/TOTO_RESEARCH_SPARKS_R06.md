## Model
P(i,j)=binom(n,i)binom(m,j)/2^(n+m), for 0<=i<=n, 0<=j<=m. For a primitive integer normal (a,b), observe the complete distribution of a*i+b*j, equivalently evaluate the generating polynomial at (x,y)=(t^a,t^b). Take k distinct directions up to sign, including (1,0),(0,1). The axis marginals force every nonnegative competitor, even if initially allowed on all Z^2, to remain in the same rectangle. This support conclusion does not apply to arbitrary signed competitors outside it.

## Kernel and exact threshold
Set Bx=sum |b| and By=sum |a| over the directions. Write r_+=max(r,0), r_-=max(-r,0).
F_(a,b)=x^(b_+)y^(a_-)-x^(b_-)y^(a_+).
The kernel of substitution into the Laurent ring is generated by this binomial: in Laurent coordinates the primitive exponent vector (b,-a) extends to a unimodular basis, reducing the substitution kernel to one variable minus 1. Intersecting with the polynomial ring gives the same divisibility condition since F is not divisible by x or y. Different primitive normals up to sign yield nonassociate irreducibles. Thus a polynomial difference invisible to all selected projections is precisely a multiple of H=product F_(a,b).

The bidegree of H is (Bx,By), and separate degrees add under multiplication. On the rectangle the kernel dimension is therefore
K=max(0,n-Bx+1)*max(0,m-By+1).
This characterizes the affine solution space; the positive Pascal law is an interior point. If K>0, a sufficiently small nonzero real multiple of H can be added to P while preserving positivity and total mass. If K=0, no distinct competitor exists. This is a sharp identifiability result in the specified model, not for all distributions or noisy observations.

Each factor vanishes at (1,1); hence H has total vanishing order at least k there. All raw mixed moments E[I^u J^v] with u+v<k agree, because the Euler differential operators of total degree below k annihilate H at (1,1). Since P(1,1)=Q(1,1)=1, the difference of convolution powers Q^r-P^r factors by Q-P; projections and these low-order mixed moments agree for all positive integer r. At the first differing total degree, moment gaps scale by r.

## Four-direction square example
Normals: (1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,-1). Bx=By=3. The first ambiguous square is n=m=3, i.e. 4 by 4 cells. For all n=m=s, K=max(0,s-2)^2.
H=(1-y)(x-1)(x-y)(1-xy).
Take Q=P+(3/128)H, strictly positive. Rows i=0..3, columns j=0..3, multiplied by 128:
P:
2 6 6 2
6 18 18 6
6 18 18 6
2 6 6 2
Q:
2 9 3 2
3 18 18 9
9 18 18 3
2 3 9 2
All four full line-sum sets and mixed moments through total degree 3 agree. The (3,1) mixed moment differs by 9/64. Normal (1,2) distinguishes the example. With that additional full projection the bidegree rises to (5,4), so all square sizes s<=4 are uniquely identifiable, while ambiguity returns at s=5. Axis and diagonal sum observations are exact, not physical tomography.
