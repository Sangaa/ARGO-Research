# R01 — Scope, prior art, and corrections

**27 November 2026 · SESSION-DERIVED · NON-CANONICAL · REQUIRES VALIDATION**

## Owner-originated direction

The owner directed the inquiry toward the need variable itself: a higher-level need may be achieved through smaller required states; those states can require alternative or jointly necessary subgoals, actions, prerequisites and resources. The owner then asked us to examine external signals and cascading effects, including how rumors and market movement might change perceived need. These are prompts and examples, not evidence that a proposed causal relation is true.

The working records distinguish a desired state, the present state, an assessed gap, a context and horizon, priority, confidence, evidence, means, conditions, resources and observed completion. The distinction between achieving a goal and completing a means is essential: finishing a course does not by itself demonstrate the skill it was meant to produce.

## Scope correction

The early idea “need as a measurable gap” overlaps established work on goals/requirements decomposition and Risk Information Seeking and Processing (RISP). RISP represents perceived information insufficiency relative to a sufficiency threshold and relates it to information seeking and processing. Later studies manipulate sufficiency thresholds and study misinformation exposure. We therefore make no novelty claim for the general gap construct, multi-goal information need, rumor repetition, or rumor-related price response.

The specific candidate now under review is narrower: in a controlled fictional market, does a price trace produced by one group’s responses to an unverified rumor alter a new group’s perceived information sufficiency or independent verification compared with a matched fixed-price condition, when no new fundamental evidence is supplied?

## Prior art and what each source supports

| Literature | Supported statement | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Griffin, Dunwoody & Neuwirth (1999), RISP | Information insufficiency relative to perceived sufficiency is an established construct associated with information seeking/processing | Does not establish the proposed market-price feedback experiment |
| Hwang & Jeong (2016) | Information sufficiency and current knowledge have been experimentally manipulated in a risk communication context | Does not establish generalization to investors or prices |
| Kim et al. (2020) | Survey study links misinformation exposure, perceived information insufficiency, and seeking/avoidance patterns in a health context | Survey evidence does not establish causal order; not a market experiment |
| Fung et al. (2026) | Goal-sensitive forms of information insufficiency have been developed and validated in a COVID-19 context | Blocks novelty claims for multi-goal sufficiency; does not test price traces |
| Jia et al. (2020) | Social-media activity around merger rumors is associated with market-price responses in a defined sample | Does not by itself measure each participant’s belief, need or verification behavior |
| Décamps & Lovo (2006); Cipriani & Guarino (2005); Shachat & Srinivasan (2022) | Internal prices and learning from trades are established subjects; price signals may reflect social learning rather than new fundamentals | Not the same as the proposed rumor-generated trace paired with RISP outcomes |
| “The effects of public disclosures and information acquisition on price informativeness…” (2025) | Experimental financial-market literature studies disclosure and information acquisition | Direct equivalence to the proposed rumor-to-price-to-sufficiency sequence remains unverified |

The absence of an exact match in this initial search is not evidence that no match exists. The broader constructs and several adjacent mechanisms are clearly established.

## Corrections retained

- An early general question about representing multiple kinds of dependency was too broad and combined unrelated systems. Recent work already directly models typed evidence-dependence graphs, effective independent evidence and bounded confidence propagation; that broad novelty candidate is withdrawn.
- Aftermarket spare-parts forecasting is a separate application. Installed-base forecasting, maintenance data, warranty data and cross-system record matching have substantial prior art. We do not fold that application into this market-information question.
- R03 initially misattributed a rumor-diffusion paper; the living record corrects it to Pröllochs, Bär & Feuerriegel (2021). The earlier attribution remains preserved in the Library history.
- An early statement suggesting no direct measurement of information need was too broad. RISP and later validated measures already do this. The corrected claim is limited to the market-price-feedback combination, whose coverage remains under review.
- “Butterfly effect” is treated as a prompt to inspect propagation or interaction, not proof of chaos, nonlinear sensitivity or a causal cascade.

## Evidence class and limits

- **READ:** cited prior studies; claims are limited to the study designs and populations described in their publications.
- **USER_REPORTED:** owner’s motivation and examples.
- **DERIVED:** the separation of nodes and the proposed feedback sequence.
- **UNAVAILABLE:** new experimental or field results; none have been collected.
- **Originality:** unresolved. No claim of scientific novelty, human peer review, or empirical validation.
