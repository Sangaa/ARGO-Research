## Probe A: aftermarket spare-parts planning

The owner gave a high-level example involving a previously sold vehicle model, its serviceable installed base, failures/warranty claims, suppliers, factory, transport, storage and lead times. This use case separates:

- causal demand generation: installed base, age/use/reliability, failures, repair or replacement;
- observations: service reports and warranty claims, which may be incomplete, delayed or duplicated;
- fulfillment and decision variables: lead time, available stock, orders, backlog and service policy.

Forecast demand is not the same variable as net reorder quantity. Lead time defines the demand horizon to cover; it does not cause a part to fail. Claims are observations, not a census of every failure. Established installed-base and maintenance forecasting literature, warranty-data-maturation work, and a 2026 ERP/FSM spare-parts study already cover close pieces of this application. This example is not the target research system for R01–R02.

## Probe B: source lineage and apparent corroboration

A separate owner-provided analogy prompted examination of how claims are copied, sourced and granted credibility. We record no historical or religious finding from that analogy. The generic research question “can typed source-dependence graphs prevent repeated roots from being counted as independent evidence?” is not retained as a novelty claim: 2026 work on typed evidence-dependence graphs and provenance-aware evidence interactions directly overlaps it.

## Correction

These probes share an abstract concern with dependencies, but have different objects and outcomes: part-demand forecasting/inventory decisions versus evidence provenance/confidence. Putting them in one model without a common measurable outcome was a classification error. They remain useful examples and prior-art checks; neither replaces the main information-need/price-feedback question.
